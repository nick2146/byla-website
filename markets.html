How it works

From curiosity to informed decisions.

Build familiarity with markets through a simple, educational paper-trading experience.

01 / EXPLORE

Research companies.

Learn what companies do, how they earn revenue, and why news and economic conditions can affect share prices.

02 / SIMULATE

Build a virtual portfolio.

Use simulated funds to practise buying and selling in a learning environment. No real trades or deposits take place.

03 / REFLECT

Review your decisions.

Consider diversification, volatility, risk, and the difference between short-term price changes and long-term business performance.

START HERE · BEGINNER GUIDE

Your first virtual portfolio, step by step.

Learn the process before placing a simulated trade. There is no need to use real money to practise financial thinking.

01

Choose a company to study

Start with a business you understand. Read what it sells, who its customers are, and how it earns revenue.

02

Write down your reason

Before a simulated trade, note the evidence behind your decision and what could prove your idea wrong.

03

Understand the risk

Consider how much of your virtual portfolio depends on one company or one industry. Diversification does not eliminate risk.

04

Review, don’t just react

Check your portfolio performance and transaction history. Compare the outcome with your original reasoning instead of judging only by profit or loss.

THE MARKET IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Four concepts worth knowing.

Short definitions to keep beside you as you explore the simulator.

01 / OWNERSHIP

Share

A unit of ownership in a company. A share price can rise or fall as expectations and market conditions change.

02 / PERFORMANCE

Portfolio value

The combined value of the investments held in a portfolio, plus any cash balance included in the account.

03 / CHANGE

Return

The gain or loss over a period, usually shown as a dollar amount or percentage compared with a starting value.

04 / RISK

Diversification

Spreading investments across different companies or sectors to avoid depending on a single holding. It cannot prevent all losses.
