01 / Finance & accounting

Know what the numbers mean.

A business can have lots of sales and still struggle if its costs are too high or it runs short of cash.

Revenue

Money earned from selling goods or services before subtracting costs.

Expenses

Costs required to operate, such as rent, supplies, wages, and advertising.

Profit

What remains after expenses are subtracted from revenue.

Profit margin

Profit expressed as a percentage of revenue: profit ÷ revenue × 100%.

Quick example

A small business earns $2,000 in revenue and has $1,500 in expenses. Its profit is $500 and its profit margin is 25%.

Case tip: Explain what the numbers mean for the decision. Don't just list calculations—connect them to the business problem.

02 / Marketing & entrepreneurship

Start with the customer.

Good marketing starts by understanding who a business serves and why those customers would choose its offer.

Target market

The specific group of customers a business aims to serve.

Value proposition

A clear explanation of the benefit a customer gets and why the offer is useful.

Product

The good, service, or experience offered to customers.

Price, place & promotion

What customers pay, where they can buy, and how they learn about the offer.

Put it into practice

Imagine a school lunch business. Before choosing social media ads, define the likely customer, their needs, their budget, and what makes the offer different from other options.

03 / Business case framework

A five-step case method.

A strong case response identifies the real problem, weighs alternatives, and recommends a specific action supported by evidence.

  1. Define the problem.State the main decision the business needs to make. Separate symptoms from root causes.
  2. Pull out the evidence.Identify the most useful facts, customer insights, financial figures, and constraints in the brief.
  3. Generate options.Compare at least two realistic approaches. Consider cost, risk, timing, and likely impact.
  4. Choose and justify.Make one clear recommendation and explain why it is stronger than the alternatives.
  5. Plan execution and measurement.Set out the first steps, timeline, approximate budget, and metrics that show whether the plan is working.

04 / Presentations & public speaking

Make every slide earn its place.

Lead with the answer

State your recommendation early so the audience understands the direction of your pitch.

Keep slides focused

Use clear headings, readable text, and one main point per slide.

Show the evidence

Use relevant figures, simple charts, or customer insights to support key claims.

Prepare for questions

Know your assumptions, costs, risks, and the reasons you rejected other options.

Simple five-slide structure

1. Problem and objective · 2. Key insights · 3. Recommendation · 4. Execution and financials · 5. Risks, metrics, and expected impact.

Adapt the structure to your assignment or competition rules. For BYLA's competition, follow the official rulebook.
